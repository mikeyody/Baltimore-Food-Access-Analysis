## Tract vintage summary and crosswalk plan

| Source | Vintage | Action |
|--------|---------|--------|
| USDA LRAM 2019 | 2010 | Crosswalk to 2020 using the Census relationship file |
| USDA SRAM 2025 | 2020 | None |
| CDC PLACES 2025 | 2020 | None |
| ACS 2020-2024 | 2020 | None |
| TIGER/Line 2024 | 2020 | None (this is the master tract list) |
| SNAP retailers, Open Baltimore | points | Spatial join to 2020 tracts |

**Crosswalk method (implemented in Phase 2):** the relationship file lists every piece
where a 2010 tract overlaps a 2020 tract, with the land area of that piece
(`AREALAND_PART`). For each 2020 tract we weight each overlapping 2010 tract by
`AREALAND_PART / AREALAND_TRACT_20` (share of the 2020 tract's land that came from that
2010 tract). Counts (e.g. low-access population) are allocated by the share of the
*2010* tract's land; flags become a weighted share, and a 2020 tract is flagged when
at least 50% of its land comes from flagged 2010 tracts. Area weighting assumes
people are spread evenly within each tract piece; this is documented as a limitation.

## 1. USDA Food Access Research Atlas - Large Retailer Access Map (LRAM, formerly FARA), 2019

- **Publisher:** USDA Economic Research Service
- **URL:** https://www.ers.usda.gov/media/5627/2019-large-retailer-access-map-lram-formerly-known-as-the-food-access-research-atlas-fara-data.zip?v=28511
- **Release:** 2019 data (store list as of June 2019; 2014-18 ACS); published April 2021
- **Tract vintage:** **2010** census tracts -> crosswalked to 2020 tracts in Phase 2
- **Rows:** 72,531 total, 200 in Baltimore City; 147 columns
- **Key columns:** `CensusTract`, `Urban`, `Pop2010`, `LowIncomeTracts`, `LILATracts_1And10`, `LILATracts_halfAnd10`, `LATracts_half`, `LATracts1`, `lapop1`, `lalowi1`, `lahunv1`, `TractHUNV`
- **Known limitations:**
  - Store list is from 2019; stores have opened and closed since.
  - Uses 2010 tract boundaries, so it must be crosswalked to 2020 tracts (see below).
  - Only counts supermarkets, supercenters and large grocery stores - the classic 'food desert' definition. This is why it's our primary low-income/low-access flag.
  - Straight-line distances only (no street network).

## 2. USDA Food Access Research Atlas - SNAP-authorized Retailer Access Map (SRAM), 2025

- **Publisher:** USDA Economic Research Service
- **URL:** https://www.ers.usda.gov/media/29395/2025-snap-authorized-retailer-access-map-sram-data.zip?v=49497
- **Release:** 2025 data (STARS store list June 2025; 2020 Census; 2020-24 ACS); published July 2026
- **Tract vintage:** **2020** census tracts (no crosswalk needed)
- **Rows:** 84,119 total, 199 in Baltimore City; 297 columns
- **Key columns:** `CensusTract20`, `Urban`, `POP2020`, `LowIncomeTracts`, `PovertyRate`, `SD_SRAM_LILATracts_1And10`, `SD_SRAM_LILATracts_halfAnd10`, `SD_SRAM_LATracts_half`, `SD_SRAM_lapophalf`, `SD_SRAM_lahunvhalf`, `DD_* (driving-distance equivalents)`
- **Known limitations:**
  - Counts **every** SNAP-authorized store (convenience, dollar, specialty stores included), so 'access' here does not mean access to a full-service grocery store. In dense Baltimore almost every tract is near some SNAP store, so SRAM flags very few tracts. Used as a secondary/comparison measure only.
  - Three separate tables (general, straight-line, driving) joined on CensusTract20.
  - Brand new product (July 2026); ERS documentation may still be evolving.

## 3. CDC PLACES: Local Data for Better Health, Census Tract Data, 2025 release

- **Publisher:** Centers for Disease Control and Prevention
- **URL:** https://data.cdc.gov/d/cwsq-ngmh (API: https://data.cdc.gov/resource/cwsq-ngmh.csv)
- **Release:** 2025 release (BRFSS 2023 for these measures; data updated Dec 2025). Newest available.
- **Tract vintage:** **2020** census tracts
- **Rows:** 792 total, 792 in Baltimore City; 24 columns
- **Key columns:** `locationname (tract GEOID)`, `measureid`, `data_value`, `low_confidence_limit`, `high_confidence_limit`, `totalpopulation`, `year`
- **Known limitations:**
  - Model-based small-area estimates, not direct measurements. Tract values are partly predicted from demographics (age, sex, race, poverty), which builds in some correlation with income - relevant for the Phase 3 regression.
  - CDC cautions against using PLACES to evaluate local programs or policies.
  - Long format (one row per tract x measure); pivoted to wide in Phase 2.
  - Tracts with no/very low population have no estimates.
